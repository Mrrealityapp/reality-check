Your Reality Check starts with one honest story.

I’m Chris, a disabled veteran who has lived homelessness more than once, survived the system, and spent the last decade quietly helping others through crisis on a disability check. No board. No big salaries. Just lived experience, radical transparency, and a promise: every dollar is treated like it came from someone who couldn’t afford to lose it.

Reality Check exists to rebuild trust where it’s been shattered — for people, for animals, and for anyone who’s tired of wondering where their support really goes.

What Reality Check actually does

Reality Check is not a feel‑good slogan. It’s a working promise built on three pillars:

  • People in crisis: Helping individuals and families facing homelessness, trauma, and impossible choices — with direct, practical support and no judgment.
  • Homeless veterans: Standing with those who served and were left behind, partnering with boots‑on‑the‑ground organizations to reach those the system ignores.
  • Animals in need: Working alongside humane societies, shelters, and animal welfare groups so that no one has to choose between their survival and their animal’s safety.

Everything is documented, explained, and open to questions. If something doesn’t make sense, you get an answer — not a marketing line.

Meet Charlie – the heart of Reality Check

Charlie isn’t a mascot. He’s my daily reminder that connection is what keeps us going. His face is the first one I see when I wake up and the presence that sits with me while I write, design, and answer messages from people in crisis.

Charlie is here on this page because I want you to see who sits next to me when decisions are made about how your support is used. If you feel calmer seeing him, you already understand why he matters.

Reality Check now intentionally includes animals and animal welfare partners in its mission, because nobody should have to lose the one being that kept them alive through their darkest days.

Stories, not statistics

For more than a decade, I’ve been the person people call when everything falls apart. Sometimes that means listening at 2 a.m. Sometimes it means finding a motel room, food, or a ride before the temperature drops below freezing.

You won’t see glossy “impact numbers” here. You’ll see real stories — with identities protected — that show exactly how support is used and why it was needed in the first place.

Reality Check is built for people who don’t just want to feel generous — they want to know their support actually changed something.
